# CBDR Privacy Policy

CBDR has no accounts, advertising, tracking, analytics SDKs or developer-operated server. Training runs on your device. Apart from Apple's own iOS frameworks, such as Apple Maps and the rating prompt described below, the only code in CBDR that was not written for it is two open-source graphics libraries, three.js and earcut, which ship inside the app and run offline. Tide and current lookups contact NOAA and CHS directly, and the map uses Apple Maps. You can choose to share records, open external links, send feedback email or submit an App Store rating. Everything that leaves your device, and everything the app keeps on it, is described below.

## What the app stores locally

Training settings: display options, scenario preferences and thresholds, a few remembered tool choices (the ship-drill view and the tide-map selection tool), and one marker recording that a one-time update to the default risk-of-collision distance has been applied. Your score for the Rules of the Road test you are taking is held for that test; CBDR keeps no history of past solo sessions.

If you turn on practice tracking: one line for each Rules of the Road question you answer in Rules Practice while tracking is on — which box it is in, the day you last answered it, and how many times you have attempted and missed it. It keeps no session scores and does not record which answer you picked or when you answered, beyond the day of your last attempt. The Rules Quiz and group tests are not recorded. Turning tracking off stops new recording but keeps what is already stored; Reset Practice Progress removes the practice record.

If you host a group test: participant names, scores, missed questions, picked answers and app-leave counts are stored on the hosting device, including after you close the host screen, until you tap Discard Test and Results and confirm, or delete the app. They do not expire on their own. If temporary class exports cannot be cleaned up or a share is still in progress, the test and its results stay saved and the app offers Retry export cleanup, after which you must tap Discard Test and Results again. When CBDR leaves the foreground with a test open on the hosting device, or with a finished result on screen on a participant's device, the whole screen is covered before iOS takes its app-switcher picture, so names and scores are not in it. Group-test sharing and exports are described below.

If you compose tests: each saved test's title, creation date and items (bank question numbers, the number that generates each calculation, and the text of your own questions with their choices and answer key) are stored on the hosting device until you delete the test or the app. Nothing about participants is stored with a saved test.

If you build scenarios: each saved scenario's name and number, own ship's course and speed, its scenario settings, each contact's position, course, speed, type and any name you enter, and the date and time you last saved it are stored in the app. Loading a scenario code that someone else made runs that scenario on the scope and is not written to your library; its range scale and risk-of-collision limits, and for a generated code its contact count and restricted-visibility choice, replace your saved training settings, while its name and contact names are not kept. Tide and current predictions you save are kept for offline use until you delete them, together with the station they came from, the dates and settings they were fetched with, and the date and time they were fetched and saved.

Resume keeps the identifiers of the last three training activities opened (hosting or joining a group test, building a scenario and Settings are not recorded), with no times, counts or session scores. Rating prompt timing keeps the date and time of the first counted day of use, the date and time of the last counted day, a count capped at five days of use, and the date and time of the last request. It records no activities or scores.

There is no in-app control that clears the Resume list, the rating-prompt record or your settings; you can change settings but not erase them. Deleting CBDR removes all of the above from the device. Backups are covered below.

## Tides, currents and maps

Live predictions require internet access. The first time you open the Select Area map in a Tides & Currents visit, again whenever you return to that map after the station lists could not be loaded or their download was interrupted, and when you tap Try Again, your device downloads the complete NOAA CO-OPS tide and current station lists and the complete Canadian Hydrographic Service (CHS) station list, whichever area you go on to look at. For a US user this includes a request to a Government of Canada server. CBDR finds the stations inside the area you select on your device; the box or outline you draw is not sent to NOAA, CHS or anyone else, though Apple Maps receives requests for the map area on screen, as described below.

When you open a station, and again when you change its dates, interval or current depth or tap Retry, your device requests that one station's predictions directly from NOAA CO-OPS for US stations or from CHS for Canadian stations. A NOAA request carries the kind of prediction, the station number, the date and time range, the interval, time basis and units, plus the datum for tides or the depth-bin number for currents. A CHS request carries the station, the date range and the prediction type; CHS answers at most seven days per request, so a longer range is fetched as a series of requests. NOAA requests carry the label "application=CBDR", and iOS adds its standard headers to every request: one names the app (CBDR), its build number and the iOS networking and system versions, and another states a preferred language. Both services can therefore tell a request came from CBDR. CBDR adds no name, account or device identifier. There is no developer server in between.

Like other internet services, NOAA (a US government agency) and CHS (Fisheries and Oceans Canada) receive your IP address and request metadata and may keep access logs under their own policies. CBDR has no data-sharing or data-processing agreement with either service (the CHS data licence Sean accepted covers only the use of CHS predictions), does not control those logs and cannot see or delete them. Saved predictions work offline.

CBDR sends these requests through a separate network session that keeps no web cache and accepts no cookies, so the requests themselves leave no record on your device of which stations or dates you looked up. Earlier versions let iOS keep a session cookie that CHS sets; CBDR 2.3 deletes it when it launches. Separately, CBDR keeps the most recent station lists it downloaded in its own cache, so stations still show if a later download fails; these are public lists, the only thing they reveal about you is the date they were saved, and they are not included in backups. Deleting CBDR removes that cache.

The Select Area map is Apple Maps. While it is on screen, your device requests map data from Apple for the area shown — first Massachusetts Bay, where the map always opens, then wherever you pan and zoom — and Apple handles those requests under its own privacy policy. CBDR never asks for your device's location; you move the map yourself.

The Source and Licence links on Vessel Identification cards and the Image Credits screen open only when you tap them, outside CBDR in your web browser or in an app that has claimed that address. Each is a fixed address built into the app, at Wikimedia Commons, Creative Commons, DVIDS or the Japan Ministry of Defense; the site you open receives your IP address and the page address under its own policy. CBDR adds nothing to the link and, apart from the Legal notice link Apple Maps places on the Select Area map, has no other links to outside websites.

The animated and 3-D explainers (Shiphandling, Waves, Weather, Radar and the man-overboard lessons) run from files inside the app, in a web view that is blocked from connecting to the internet and keeps no web data between visits. The Shiphandling, Waves and Weather drawing code includes the open-source three.js and earcut libraries; none of these explainers collects anything.

## Group tests and sharing

The test code an instructor shows or shares, as a QR code or as text, holds only a random test number, the question count, which rules the test uses, a random number that picks the questions and a format version. It contains no names. In CBDR 2.3 and later an instructor can also host a composed test; its code carries the test itself: the bank numbers of the questions chosen, the number that generates each calculation, and the full text of any questions the instructor wrote, with their choices and answer key, so the same test can be hosted again on any device. It contains no names and no results. A long composed code is shared as text rather than as a QR code.

A group test asks for a name or an identifier your instructor accepts. CBDR does not verify your identity. When you finish, the result code contains the test number, your name, your score, the questions you missed with the answer you picked for each (or that you left it blank), and the number of times the app entered the background during the test. For a question answered by typing a figure, marking a drawing or drawing a track, the code records only that it was missed; what you entered stays on your device, on the finished screen, for your own review, and is not kept after you leave that screen. Showing the QR code lets the instructor receive that record directly, without a server. Result and scenario codes are encoded, not encrypted: anyone who can see or photograph a code on a screen, or who receives a copy, can read its contents.

The app-leave count records background transitions, not what you did outside CBDR. Interruptions can have innocent causes. The count and result are advisory records, not proof of cheating or independently authenticated exam results. The instructor receives the count with your result and can include it in reports.

You can also copy or share codes. In CBDR 2.3 and later, every copy CBDR makes itself — the Copy button beside a code, Copy on the ideal contact report, and the feedback draft when no mail app can open it — goes to the clipboard for this device only and expires after five minutes; other software allowed to read the clipboard may still read it in that time, and earlier versions used the normal clipboard. Two copies are made by iOS rather than by CBDR and use the normal clipboard, which does not expire and which your clipboard and Handoff settings may carry to your other Apple devices: the share sheet's own Copy action, and any text you select and copy with the system menu (including the ideal contact report, the scenario code on the Scenario Library screen and Chart No. 1 text). A contact report or a scenario code from a built scenario can include the contact names entered for it.

The system share sheet sends what you share to the app, person or service you choose — AirDrop, Messages, Mail, Files, a printer or another app — which may use the internet or cloud storage. CBDR never picks the destination and never sends anything itself; the app or service you choose does the sending. You can share test and result codes, class PDF and CSV records, printable problem sets, paper tests and their answer keys, codes for scenarios you built, tide and current CSV files and the blank Excel templates. A code for a generated scenario contains only a format version, the random number that generates it, its settings and which library scenario it came from. A code for a scenario you built contains its name and number, own ship's course and speed, the scenario settings, and each contact's bearing, range, course, speed, type and identification and fog-signal settings, plus any contact names you entered. Choose recipients accordingly.

An instructor can export class records. The PDF contains the test details and class totals; a signable roster with each participant's name, score, pass or fail with a remediation-required mark, and app-leave count when above zero; the highest and lowest scorers by name, listing everyone tied; a table of every question with its percent correct and how many missed it; and a copy of each participant's test that shows, for each missed question, the answer picked (or that it was left blank) where the result code carried it together with the correct answer, and for every other question the correct answer from the question bank. The CSV contains the test details and class totals, each participant's name, score, pass or fail, remediation mark, app-leave count and missed question numbers, and a table of every question with its percent correct; it does not include picked answers. In CBDR 2.3 and later, the app tries to delete its temporary working copy once sharing finishes or is cancelled and the share sheet closes, and shows a message with a retry button if it cannot. It also attempts stale-export cleanup when launched or when the host screen opens. Discard Test and Results removes temporary class exports before clearing the saved session; if cleanup fails or sharing is still active, the test and its results stay saved and the app offers Retry export cleanup, after which you must tap Discard Test and Results again to delete them. Earlier versions may leave temporary exports until they are overwritten or removed by the operating system. Documents saved or shared elsewhere are not erased by these controls. The instructor or institution controls its copies and any required retention; contact them about a class record.

Export CSV on a station screen opens the share sheet with a file of the predictions on screen. The file contains the station's name and number, the data source (with the CHS licence notice for Canadian stations), the datum, for currents the depth bin and the mean flood and ebb directions, the interval, time basis, units and date range, the time the data was fetched, and the predicted values. It contains nothing about you — no name, account or device identifier of yours — but the station, date range and fetch time show what was looked up and when. CBDR writes the file to its temporary folder only after you tap Export CSV, when the share sheet asks for it, and removes leftover export files when you leave the station screen (those older than five minutes) and when it next launches. The blank Excel templates are fixed files, the same for every user, containing CBDR's layout and formulas and the CHS notice, and nothing about you.

Share PDF on the Maneuvering Board's Printable Problem Sets screen opens the share sheet with a PDF of generated problems, a blank maneuvering board for each, and, unless you turn the answer key off, the answers and constructions. The file carries the set code, the problem types, the problem count and the date it was made, and nothing about you. CBDR writes it to the same temporary folder as class exports only when you tap Share PDF, and removes it the way it removes class exports: when sharing finishes or is cancelled and the share sheet closes, and at the next launch.

Paper Test and Answer Key on Build Test's review open the share sheet with a PDF of the test for printing: its title, version code and questions with their pictures, and for the key each answer with its source or worked solution. The files carry nothing about any participant. CBDR writes them to the same temporary folder as class exports only when you tap one, and removes them the way it removes class exports: when sharing finishes or is cancelled and the share sheet closes, and at the next launch.

## Storage, backups and deletion

Depending on your Apple or computer backup settings, device backups may include CBDR's local storage: training settings and remembered choices; the Resume list and the rating-prompt record; the practice record, if you have ever turned tracking on and have not since used Reset Practice Progress; a hosted group test that has not been discarded, with participant names, scores, missed questions, picked answers and app-leave counts; your scenario library, its last-good copy and any preserved recovery files; and saved tide and current predictions. Cached station lists and temporary exports are not backed up. Sean has no access to your device or computer backups. Deleting CBDR removes its local app storage, but does not erase existing backups, copied codes, shared records or exported documents. Restoring a backup may restore app data.

Use Reset Practice Progress, Discard Test and Results, or the scenario and saved-prediction deletion controls to remove the corresponding local records. Scenario deletion is unavailable while the library file cannot be read. On iPad, close CBDR's other windows first: a window that was open before a reset or discard still holds the earlier practice record or group test and can save it again. Manage backups and exported copies separately through the services or devices that hold them.

In CBDR 2.3 and later, the scenario library keeps a last-good copy for recovery. After you edit or rename a scenario, that copy holds the earlier version, including its earlier name and contact names, until your next change to the library; deleting a scenario also removes it from that copy. If CBDR cannot safely use the library file (because it cannot be read, or because another CBDR window changed it) and you confirm Restore Copy, CBDR first saves the current file under a new, separate name so data is not silently lost; if the restore then fails, that saved file is still kept, so each confirmed attempt can add another. Those preserved files may contain scenario and contact names, are not cleared by individual scenario deletion, and remain in local app storage until CBDR is deleted. Existing device backups may retain earlier copies.

## Microphone, speech and camera

Microphone and speech recognition are used only when you tap the microphone to speak a contact report. iOS asks for speech-recognition and microphone permission the first time you tap it, before CBDR checks whether on-device recognition is available; the speech alert shows CBDR's stated purpose together with Apple's standard wording that speech data is sent to Apple, which iOS shows for every app. CBDR starts listening only when iOS reports that on-device speech recognition is available for US English, and it marks every request on-device only, which Apple documents as keeping the audio from being sent over the network. The microphone closes when you tap stop or close the report sheet. CBDR grades the spoken or typed text on the device; CBDR keeps the text and its grade only in memory while the report sheet is open, never saves or sends them, and discards them when you close the sheet. If on-device speech recognition is unavailable, type your report instead.

The dictation key on the iOS keyboard is part of iOS, not CBDR. If you dictate into a CBDR text field, Apple processes your voice under its own policy and your Siri and Dictation settings.

The camera is used to scan QR codes and in the Star Finder. The QR scanner starts when you open Join a Test or tap Scan Result on the host screen, and the Star Finder's camera starts when you confirm its warning screen; nothing else opens the camera. Frames are processed on the device and discarded; no image is ever stored or uploaded by CBDR. The Star Finder never asks where you are: it identifies patterns of stars. It examines about one frame per second and stops the camera when it finds a match or you leave. If you tap the dim button on its warning screen, the Star Finder sets your screen brightness to 10 percent, keeps your previous level in memory only, and puts it back when you tap again or close the Star Finder.

## App Store ratings and Apple diagnostics

CBDR may ask Apple to show its native rating prompt. Rating is optional. Apple handles ratings and reviews; CBDR does not receive your response in the app or send your training data with the request. A review you publish can be visible to the developer and others on the App Store.

CBDR contains no analytics or crash-reporting code. If you have turned on Share With App Developers in iOS Settings (Privacy & Security, then Analytics & Improvements), Apple may give Sean crash, hang and energy reports and usage statistics for CBDR through App Store Connect and Xcode. Apple collects these under its own privacy policy, and CBDR adds nothing to them. Apple also gives Sean App Store download figures by country from its own store records. If you ever test a beta through TestFlight, Apple's TestFlight terms apply to the crash reports and feedback you send there.

## Feedback and privacy questions

Sean Bowman is personally responsible for CBDR and answers support at sean.cbdr@gmail.com, a Google Gmail account. The FEEDBACK buttons, Send Feedback in Settings and Email Us in Vessel Identification open Apple's mail composer inside CBDR, with a draft addressed to sean.cbdr@gmail.com; Mail's Cc/Bcc, From line names, or lets you check, the account it will be sent from. If Apple's composer is not available (for example, Mail has no account or has been removed), CBDR hands the same draft to your default mail app, which may be a third-party app and may ask you to set up an account first. Only if iOS reports that no app can open it does CBDR copy the whole draft (address, subject and body) to this device's clipboard for five minutes, replacing what was there, and tell you so. The subject names the part of the app. The body may include the question number and text, the app's correct answer or explanation, a ship card's class and photo credit, a generated practice problem, the time zones highlighted on the map, or the drill or page you were on (from a completion card, the drill family and that you finished the session), with fixed prompts such as "My feedback:"; it never includes device or account information, your answers or your scores. You can read and edit all of it before sending, and nothing is sent unless you send it. Sending is optional and never part of training; CBDR keeps no record of whether you sent, saved or cancelled a message.

When you send, Sean and the mail providers involved receive your email address and the name on your mail account, your message, anything you attach, and whatever your mail app adds, such as a signature like "Sent from my iPhone" and technical headers that can name the mail app and iOS version. Sean uses them only to reply and to investigate and fix the app. He does not add you to any list, use your address for marketing, or sell or share your correspondence with anyone else. Email Us on the Vessel Identification screen also invites photographs or details for the ship library; anything you offer that way is used only to consider it for the library, and Sean confirms with you before publishing it or crediting you by name.

CBDR does not automatically send participant names, scores or class records to Sean. Do not include someone else's class record in support email unless you are authorized to share it.

Support emails currently have no fixed deletion schedule. Contact sean.cbdr@gmail.com to ask about access, correction or deletion of correspondence you supplied, or to raise a privacy concern. Records held only by an instructor or on your device are not available to the developer.
